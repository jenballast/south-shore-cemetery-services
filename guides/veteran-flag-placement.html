Flag placement and grave care for veterans

What actually happens at a veteran's grave on Memorial Day and Veterans Day, and how to prepare the site ahead of time.

Flags at veterans' graves are one of the more visible traditions in a South Shore cemetery every May and November, and one families sometimes lose track of once nobody local is checking on the site regularly. Here's how it actually works, and what we do for the veterans' plots we care for.

Who places the flags, and when

In most South Shore towns, small American flags at veterans' graves go in ahead of Memorial Day, typically placed in bulk by veterans' organizations, Boy Scout and Girl Scout troops, or town Veterans' Agents in the days before the last Monday in May. Veterans Day, on November 11th, sometimes sees a second smaller round of placement or flag replacement in towns that hold graveside ceremonies, though it's less universal than the Memorial Day tradition. Either way, if your family plot includes a veteran, a flag placed at the base of the stone is standard practice, not something you need to arrange privately in most cases.

Where families come in is when a specific individual placement matters to them — a flag placed by hand on the actual day, rather than left to whatever bulk placement the town does — or when the grave is in a small or older section that town crews sometimes miss.

What we do at veterans' graves

If your family's plot includes a veteran, we'll place a flag for Memorial Day or Veterans Day at no charge — just let us know when you book any other service, or reach out specifically for this. It takes a minute, and it's the right thing to do while we're already standing there. We also remove and properly retire flags once they've weathered past a dignified condition, rather than leaving a faded or torn flag in place through the following season.

Several cemeteries we work in hold real concentrations of veterans' graves — Kingston's Old Burying Ground has 22 local veterans of the Revolution, Rockland's Mount Pleasant Cemetery holds the G.A.R. Soldiers' Lot and the 1913 monument to the Unreturned Soldiers, and Hull Village Cemetery sees local soldiers' graves decorated every Memorial Day. In these plots especially, flag condition and stone condition tend to go together — a family stone that hasn't been cleaned in years is often in a section where nobody's tending to much else either.

Cleaning the stone before the holiday

Flags look best against a legible, cared-for stone rather than one dulled by lichen or algae. If you want the site looking its best for Memorial Day, the timing matters: a proper cleaning takes about three visits over several weeks, so it needs to start well ahead of the holiday, not the week before. As a rule of thumb, beginning in March gives enough time for a full cleaning to be finished before Memorial Day weekend. Starting in early May is usually too late for the stone to look fully restored by the day itself, though a single tidy-up visit can still make a real difference.

If you're not sure a flag has been placed

This comes up more than you'd think, especially for families who live too far away to check in person. If you'd like us to confirm a flag is in place, place one ourselves, or send a photo either way, just tell us the cemetery and roughly where the plot is. It's a small thing to check, and for a family who can't be there, knowing it's been done matters.
