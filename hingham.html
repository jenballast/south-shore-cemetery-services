Serving Hingham

Care for the places that hold your memories

Hingham's cemeteries include both historic burying grounds and established town sections. Shaded plots hold moisture, and moisture feeds the biological growth that darkens a stone — north-facing and tree-covered sites almost always need more frequent attention.

Most cemeteries mow the lawn. They don't clean the stone, clear the debris from around the base, or put flowers out in the spring. That part has always fallen to families — and when you live an hour away, or a plane ride away, it quietly stops happening. That's the gap we fill.

What We Do

Services available in Hingham

Stone Cleaning & Restoration

We use D/2 Biological Solution — the same stone-safe formula used on national monuments — applied over a minimum of three visits. No pressure washers, no wire brushes, no acids. Those methods cause permanent damage to granite, marble, and slate alike.

Regular Site Maintenance

Leaves, sticks, and debris accumulate around foot stones and base edges year-round. We handle the careful housekeeping that keeps a site looking intentional rather than merely mowed.

Seasonal Plantings & Décor

Spring blooms, summer color, autumn arrangements, and Christmas wreaths — with removal included at the end of the season, in line with each cemetery's own regulations.

Common Questions

Hingham questions we hear most

Which Hingham cemeteries do you cover?

Hingham Cemetery, Hingham Centre Cemetery, High Street, Fort Hill, Canterbury Street, St. Paul's, and Saint John the Evangelist Memorial Garden. If yours isn't listed, ask.

The stone is under trees and always looks green. What causes that?

Shade holds moisture, and moisture feeds algae and lichen. Shaded and north-facing plots green up considerably faster than open ones. Those sites usually do best on a regular schedule rather than a one-time cleaning.

Will you follow the cemetery's rules on plantings?

Always. Each cemetery sets its own regulations on what can be planted, how tall it can be, and when decorations must come out. We work within them, so nothing we place is removed unexpectedly.
