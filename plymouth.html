Serving Plymouth

Care for the places that hold your memories

Plymouth is the largest town in Massachusetts by land area, and the town alone maintains more than thirty cemeteries — from colonial burying grounds to modern memorial sections spread from the harbor out to Cedarville. Distance within Plymouth itself is often the obstacle, never mind families who live three states away.

Most cemeteries mow the lawn. They don't clean the stone, clear the debris from around the base, or put flowers out in the spring. That part has always fallen to families — and when you live an hour away, or a plane ride away, it quietly stops happening. That's the gap we fill.

What We Do

Services available in Plymouth

Stone Cleaning & Restoration

We use D/2 Biological Solution — the same stone-safe formula used on national monuments — applied over a minimum of three visits. No pressure washers, no wire brushes, no acids. Those methods cause permanent damage to granite, marble, and slate alike.

Regular Site Maintenance

Leaves, sticks, and debris accumulate around foot stones and base edges year-round. We handle the careful housekeeping that keeps a site looking intentional rather than merely mowed.

Seasonal Plantings & Décor

Spring blooms, summer color, autumn arrangements, and Christmas wreaths — with removal included at the end of the season, in line with each cemetery's own regulations.

Common Questions

Plymouth questions we hear most

Plymouth is enormous. Do you cover the whole town?

We do. The town maintains more than thirty cemeteries spread from the harbor out to Cedarville, and we travel across them — Vine Hills, Oak Grove, Chiltonville, Manomet, Cedarville, South Pond, Plymouth County Memorial, and Burial Hill among them.

Can you find the stone if I'm not sure exactly where it is?

Usually. Give us the name, the cemetery, and any dates you have. There's a one-time stone-finding fee between $15 and $25 depending on how much searching it takes, and it only applies once.

Do you handle very old markers on Burial Hill?

We do, with extra care. Stones there date from 1680 onward and many are thin slate. We work by hand with stone-safe solution and never use anything abrasive.
