Where Else We Work

The same service, a wider radius

Our core towns have pages of their own. These are the communities we reach regularly beyond them — from the Blue Hills down through the cranberry country, and out across the Quincy and Weymouth line.

Everything works the same way here: a stone-safe cleaning applied over a minimum of three visits, debris clearing that the grounds crew doesn't do, seasonal plantings within each cemetery's own rules, and dated photographs before and after every visit.

Quincy & the Blue Hills

Northern towns

  • Quincy A city of granite, and it shows in the monuments. Mount Wollaston Cemetery on Sea Street (1855, on the National Register) holds some of the finest examples anywhere of "turning" granite, a technique developed by a local Quincy firm. Also Hancock Cemetery on Hancock Street, the city's main burial ground until 1854; Pine Hill Cemetery on Willard Street; Sailors' Home Cemetery in Merrymount Park, with more than 120 Civil War veterans; and Snug Harbor Sailors' Cemetery.
  • Braintree Elm Street Cemetery, opposite the First Congregational Church, dates to 1716 and is among the oldest burial grounds in the Commonwealth — the kind of thin, early stone that needs a careful hand. Also Plain Street Cemetery on Plain Street (the town's active cemetery), Pond Street Cemetery, Dyer Hill Cemetery on Jefferson Street, and Blue Hill Cemetery, laid out in 1892 on Olmsted-influenced grounds near the Blue Hills Reservation.
  • Milton Milton Cemetery at 211 Centre Street was established in 1672 and has been in continuous use ever since — 102 acres in the garden cemetery style, on the National Register. Its Old Burying Ground sections hold Dorchester and Harvard slates from the earliest colonial period, and they are exactly the stones most at risk from aggressive cleaning.
  • Weymouth Fifteen cemeteries across town, eight under the Weymouth Cemetery Commission. Old North Burial Ground is the earliest, holding the town's first settlers — including the parents of Abigail Adams — and the site of the Soldiers' Monument. Also Ashwood Cemetery on Broad Street (1819), Fairmount Cemetery on Cedar Street, Mount Hope Cemetery in South Weymouth, Village Cemetery, Lakeview, Highland, Elmwood, and St. Francis Xavier.
  • Canton Canton Corner Cemetery is the town's historic burying ground, with roughly 4,000 recorded memorials spanning generations of Canton families. Also Knollwood Memorial Park and St. Mary's Cemetery.
  • Randolph Central Cemetery on North Street, run by the Central Cemetery Association, along with Oakland Cemetery on Oak Street, Lindwood Memorial Park, and St. Mary's. Family plots here often hold several markers across generations, which is where our multi-memorial pricing makes the most difference.
  • Holbrook Union Cemetery on South Franklin Street is the town's principal burial ground, serving Holbrook families for well over a century.

Inland & Cranberry Country

Southern and western towns

  • Abington Mount Vernon Cemetery on Central Street opened in 1852 and is the town's main burial ground, with more than 10,000 recorded memorials. Its Minister's Corner holds remains consolidated from Abington's scattered early family burying grounds. Also the Adams Street Burial Ground and several small family plots.
  • Whitman Colebrook Cemetery on Essex Street is the town's principal cemetery, run by the Colebrook Cemetery Association. Also Mount Zion Cemetery and St. James Cemetery.
  • Hanson Fern Hill Cemetery on High Street began around 1748 as the churchyard of what is now the First Congregational Church, and remains the town's only active cemetery — its earliest engraved stone dates to 1745. Hanson also holds several small historic burying grounds, including Hobart Cemetery near Franklin and Main, that have had little attention over the years.
  • Pembroke Center Cemetery sits at Pembroke Center beside the First Church, on the site where the first meeting house was built in 1708. Also Briggs Cemetery and other town burying grounds.
  • Halifax Central Cemetery is the town's main burial ground. Halifax plots are frequently wooded and shaded, which holds moisture against the stone and speeds up biological growth — these sites usually do better on a regular schedule than on one-time cleanings.
  • Plympton Hillcrest Cemetery is the town's principal cemetery, along with the Congregational Church burying ground. A small town with old stones — the kind of markers where method matters more than effort.
  • Carver Union Cemetery on South Main Street (1777) sits beside the Union Church and has been called the Mount Auburn of Plymouth County. Also Central Cemetery on Main Street behind the United Parish Church, Lakenham Cemetery off North Main Street — the town's oldest, dating to 1718 — Wenham Cemetery in East Carver (1770), and Our Lady of Lourdes.
This isn't every cemetery in every town. If the one you're looking for isn't listed, get in touch and we'll confirm we can reach it — we often travel beyond this list.
