St. Francis Xavier Cemetery, Weymouth

A parish cemetery on Route 53 since 1862 — nearly 9,500 burials, its own records search, and none of the town's rules apply.

Saint Francis Xavier Cemetery is on Washington Street — Route 53 — in Weymouth, with the entrance off Route 53. It was founded in 1862, which makes it one of the older Catholic cemeteries on this side of the South Shore, and it is a substantial one: close to 9,500 recorded burials.

It is a parish cemetery, run by St. Francis Xavier Parish, part of the St. Albert the Great and St. Francis Xavier Collaborative. Cemetery business goes to 781-337-2171 ext. 2 or info@sfxcemetery.org; the parish office is at 234 Pleasant Street, South Weymouth.

Weymouth has fifteen cemeteries, and this is not one of the town's. Eight Weymouth cemeteries fall under the Weymouth Cemetery Commission. St. Francis Xavier is not among them — it belongs to the parish, holds its own records, and the town will not have them. Note also that the cemetery keeps its own website, separate from the parish's; for church matters the collaborative is at sagsfx.org.

Finding a burial here

The cemetery runs its own online records search, which is more than most parish cemeteries offer, alongside an interment request form. Between that and the office, this is a straightforward cemetery to research from a distance.

Find a Grave indexes it thoroughly — about 9,486 memorials with roughly 93% photographed — and volunteers have posted a cemetery map there with the office's permission. The Massachusetts Historical Commission catalogs the cemetery in MACRIS as WEY806.

For the period before civil registration was consistent, a Catholic parish's sacramental registers often hold what no index does, so ask the parish rather than assuming an absent record means an absent burial. How to find an ancestor's grave on the South Shore covers the wider search.

One caution specific to this name: there are St. Francis Xavier cemeteries in Hyannis, in Vermont, in New York State and elsewhere. If you are working from a search result, confirm it says Weymouth before you drive anywhere.

If you have located a grave and cannot get to Weymouth, we can walk the section, find the stone, and photograph it for a one-time fee of $15–$25.

Plantings and decoration rules

The cemetery does not publish a rules and regulations document. Its website is deliberately spare — it exists, in its own words, to provide cemetery information to vendors and the general public, and carries the records search, an interment request form, and a map. There is no rules page, and we are not going to guess at one.

Call the cemetery at 781-337-2171 ext. 2 before planting or placing anything. If you learn the current rules, we would be glad to hear them and will add them here with a note on when they were confirmed.

It is worth knowing how wide the range is. Among the cemeteries in this section, First Parish in Norwell permits permanent beds, bulbs and shrubs; St. Mary's in Scituate, the other parish cemetery we have written up, allows annuals or perennials in a 12-inch bed but no mulch, and sets a May 1 to November 30 flag season; Hingham Cemetery permits fresh flowers for one week and nothing artificial ever. St. Mary's is probably the closest comparison here, but that is an expectation, not a rule we can point to. General guidance is in what to plant at a grave site.

Stone care here

A cemetery in continuous use since 1862 will hold mostly marble in its earliest sections and granite through the twentieth century onward, with the Victorian marble being the material most at risk from a wrong method — it is soft, it etches under acid, and it sugars at the surface once damage starts. Granite, marble, and slate covers how each behaves.

Weymouth sits at the top of the bay, so there is some salt influence, but Route 53 is inland enough that this is not a Hull or Cohasset situation. Expect a slower cycle than an exposed coastal plot, with shade and drainage at the specific grave mattering more than the town does. Coastal and shaded stone care covers how to think about frequency.

We clean with D/2 Biological Solution over a minimum of three visits — no pressure washing, no wire brushes, nothing that leaves salts behind in the stone. Why D/2 and not bleach explains the reasoning, and the three-visit process covers what happens on site.

We have not found a published rule here restricting who may clean a stone, as some cemeteries have. Because the cemetery's own site is oriented toward vendors, we would expect the office to want to know who is working on the grounds, and we would tell them before starting.

Veterans' graves

We have not found a published flag policy for St. Francis Xavier. Practice at parish cemeteries varies considerably — St. Mary's in Scituate publishes a firm May 1 to November 30 season, while others leave it to families or to local veterans' organisations. Ask the cemetery office rather than assuming. Our guide to flag placement covers the general etiquette and who to ask, and we place and retire flags at the veterans' plots we care for at no charge.

Contacting the cemetery

Saint Francis Xavier Cemetery is on Washington Street (Route 53) in Weymouth, reachable at 781-337-2171 ext. 2 or info@sfxcemetery.org, with the parish office at 234 Pleasant Street, South Weymouth, MA 02190. Its website — carrying the records search, interment request form, and cemetery map — is sfxcemetery.org. We are not affiliated with the parish or the collaborative; we are a private care service that works in the cemetery.

Weymouth is covered on More Towns We Serve, which lists the town's other burial grounds — including Old North Burial Ground, Ashwood, Fairmount, Mount Hope, and the rest.
