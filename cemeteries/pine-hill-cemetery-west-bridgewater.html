Pine Hill Cemetery, West Bridgewater

A privately run cemetery with burials predating 1850, the best index coverage of any ground we have written up — and no published rules.

Pine Hill Cemetery is on North Main Street (Route 28) in West Bridgewater, about twenty miles south of Boston. It is a privately run cemetery and a member of the New England Cemetery Association, governed by a board, with lot owners referred to as proprietors. There is a cemetery office on the grounds, and the gates are open dawn to dusk daily.

Records and permissions go to the cemetery directly at 508-583-5466 or Pinehillcemetery@comcast.net. West Bridgewater has no town cemetery department that we could find, so the town offices are not the place to ask about a Pine Hill burial.

Two different Pine Hills. There is also a Pine Hill Cemetery on Willard Street in Quincy, roughly twenty-five miles north. If you are searching an index and getting results that do not match what you expect, check which town the record is filed under.

Age and size

The cemetery's own site says only that it has "served families for generations," without a founding date, and we have not found one we can stand behind. What we can say is that it is old enough to appear in the Vital Records of West Bridgewater, Massachusetts to 1850, published by the New England Historic Genealogical Society, where it is designated G.R.6. That places burials here before 1850 at the latest.

It is also large. Find a Grave indexes close to 10,000 memorials at Pine Hill, roughly 95% of them photographed and 83% carrying GPS coordinates. That is the best index coverage of any cemetery we have written up — better than Cohasset Central or St. Paul — which makes it an unusually easy cemetery to research from a distance.

The grounds are gently rolling with mature trees and walking paths, and are used by local walkers and joggers as well as by visiting families. There are water stations at two points: south of the cemetery office, and near the main gate on North Main Street. Small thing, but it matters if you are planting or tidying a plot on a summer afternoon.

A notable burial

Private James Maynard Stetson is buried here. He enlisted in Company C of the 57th Massachusetts Infantry at sixteen, giving his age as twenty-one. He was wounded at Spotsylvania Court House in May 1864, underwent multiple amputations, and died of complications at Emory General Hospital in Washington on 29 May 1864, aged seventeen. His remains were later exhumed and returned to West Bridgewater for burial.

He was born 1 March 1847.

Lots and care

Pine Hill offers estate plots for multiple family members, individual plots, and cremation options including urn burial and entombment and a columbarium memorial wall. Perpetual care is included with both estate and individual plots.

It is worth being clear about what perpetual care covers, because families often assume more than it does. Perpetual care at most cemeteries funds the general upkeep of the grounds — mowing, roads, trees, the overall appearance of the place. It does not usually mean anyone cleans your stone, tends a planting at your plot, or places flowers on a date that matters to your family. Those remain the lot owner's responsibility. If the grounds look well kept but a particular stone has gone dark with lichen, that is the distinction showing.

Plantings and decoration rules

The cemetery does not publish a rules and regulations document that we have been able to find, and we are not going to guess at one. The site refers to "standard monument regulations" for individual plots without setting them out, and a monument firm that works here notes that requirements differ by section — some sections take upright headstones, others flush or lawn-level markers only.

The one concrete scheduling detail the cemetery has posted: in 2025 it removed Christmas decorations beginning 15 March, asking proprietors to collect anything they wanted to keep before that date. A mid-March clearance is later than most cemeteries on the South Shore, several of which clear Christmas décor by 1 March or earlier — but treat it as one year's notice rather than a standing rule.

Call the office before planting or placing anything, and ask specifically about your section. If you learn the current rules, we would be glad to hear them and will add them here with a note on when they were confirmed.

For general guidance on what suits a grave site in this climate, see what to plant at a grave site. As the other cemeteries in this section show, rules vary enormously — First Parish in Norwell permits permanent beds and shrubs, while Hingham Cemetery allows fresh flowers for one week and nothing artificial at all.

Stone care here

West Bridgewater is inland, which changes the problem. Salt spray is not a factor the way it is at the coastal grounds in Hull, Cohasset, or Scituate. What an inland cemetery with mature trees and rolling ground gives you instead is shade and moisture retention, and those produce their own biological growth — algae, moss, and lichen on stones that never see the ocean. North-facing markers and anything under a tree canopy will darken fastest. Coastal and shaded stone care covers why the two situations often call for a similar schedule for different reasons.

A cemetery with pre-1850 burials will hold a range of material — early slate and marble in the older sections, granite in the newer ones — and each wants a different approach, which granite, marble, and slate covers.

We clean with D/2 Biological Solution over a minimum of three visits, with no pressure washing, no wire brushes, and nothing that leaves salts behind in the stone. Why D/2 and not bleach explains the reasoning, and the three-visit process covers what happens on site. We have not found a published rule at Pine Hill restricting who may clean a stone, but we would tell the office before working, which is one phone call.

Finding a burial here

Start with Find a Grave, where coverage is excellent, and BillionGraves as a second source. For pre-1850 research, the NEHGS Vital Records of West Bridgewater to 1850 transcribes this cemetery as G.R.6, and is the natural next step when an index comes up short.

Bear in mind that West Bridgewater has eleven recorded burial grounds, many of them small family graveyards — Alger, Ames, Cochesett, Hayward, Jerusalem, Old Graveyard, Pleasant Hill, Powder House, Thayer, and Dunbar among them. An early West Bridgewater ancestor may well be in one of those rather than at Pine Hill. How to find an ancestor's grave on the South Shore covers the wider search.

If you have located a grave here and cannot get to West Bridgewater, we can walk the section, find the stone, and photograph it for a one-time fee of $15–$25.

Contacting the cemetery

Pine Hill Cemetery is at North Main Street (Route 28), West Bridgewater, MA 02379, reachable at 508-583-5466 or Pinehillcemetery@comcast.net, and its website is pinehillwb.com. Lot purchases, burial arrangements, plot location, and questions about section rules all go through the office. We are not affiliated with the cemetery; we are a private care service that works in it.
